Overfitting occurs when a model learns the training data too well, including its noise, and therefore performs poorly on new data. Underfitting occurs when a model is too simple to capture the underlying pattern and performs badly on both training and test data.

Overfitting can be reduced by collecting more data, using regularization, applying dropout, using cross-validation and stopping training early. Balancing the two problems is known as the bias-variance trade-off.
